## Warnings (non-blocking)

### W-A (owner W3, BOSS to decide): large Index swing while events are open

- `services/api/lifeline_sim/api.py:152`: after AGE, the twin is replayed to the end of the year with its defaults already applied. The player has not answered yet.
- Until the player answers, the Index compares the player with a twin that already took the result of those events.
- `docs/index.md` says the Index "can move a little" here. Measured over 180 all-default lifetimes: 240 open-event steps move more than 5 points. The largest is +247 points (banker-london, seed 5, 2072, open `family.divorce`; the twin had already lost about £2.3M). Common triggers: `work.redundancy`, `home.rent_rise`, `family.inheritance`, `family.divorce`.
- The settled value is correct (exactly 100 for all defaults). The value shown while events are open can mislead, and the rival strip in Same Life could show it.
- Suggested fix: compare against the twin with only the defaults of events the player has already answered this year, or hide or freeze the Index while events are open.

### W-B (owner W3, balance note): Index has no cap and can reach 1,000+

- Taking the first non-default choice at every event ends lives with Index 401 to 1,124 (nurse-leeds, banker-london). R_player minus R_autopilot reaches about 100 years of starting pay.
- This matches BUILD_SPEC and ADR 0012 (no cap). It suggests that default choices are often the worse option. W3 can review event balance. Display is a UI concern.

### Notes

- The `min_income0 = 10,000` floor in R(t) is not in BUILD_SPEC. It is documented in `docs/index.md` and ADR 0012. Accepted.
- `r_player` and `r_autopilot` are rounded to 4 dp separately, so equal net worths can show a 0.0001 gap. The Index value is exact.

## Warnings (non-blocking)

### T1-W1 (owner W2): cut_budget headline number not in any fact
- `services/api/lifeline_engine/next_move.py:922`: headline "You spent £119 more than usual on transport" (homeowner-leeds) and "£30 more ... on shopping" (subscription-creep). £119 = spent - average, but no evidence `facts` holds it.
- Evidence texts are clean. If the explain verifier checks headlines against facts, it rejects these. Suggest: add `excess` to the facts of evidence 1 or 3.

### T1-W2 (owner W2): Test it changes only 1 of 3 years for mortgage moves
- `services/api/lifeline_engine/next_move.py:1003` sets `__deal_end_year = test_period.start + 1` (2022). 2021 has no events. 2022 pays the old fixed rate (1.86%) for every option. The choice applies only from 2023.
- The card's stress figure is a 5-year average from 2021. Test it shows one year of difference (£786 tracker vs fix 5y, against £2,987 a year on the card). The direction agrees (fix 5y wins in the stress period). The magnitudes do not.
- Suggest: set the deal end to the start year or extend the test period, so the choice covers more years. W3 and BOSS to decide.

### T1-W3 (owner W2): confidence of cut_budget and cancel_subscription is not twin based
- `next_move.py:906`: cut_budget confidence = share of the user's last 6 months at or under the average. `next_move.py:875`: cancel_subscription confidence = 1.0 ("A sure saving").
- ADR 0018 defines confidence as a share of (twin, period) pairs. The labels describe the real basis, so the user is not misled. Suggest an ADR note.

### T1-W4 (owner W2): council tax missing from the bills list
- `services/api/lifeline_engine/money/recurring.py:93` (`classify_cadence`): every gap must fit one cadence. Council tax is paid in 10 monthly instalments with a February-March break, so the gap of about 90 days rejects the series.
- Leeds, Lambeth, Manchester and Bristol councils appear 10 times each in the fixtures but in no persona's bills.

### T1-W5 (owner W2): rounding rule of score_guess
- `services/api/lifeline_engine/money/predict.py:506` uses Python `round` (half to even). Guess 51, actual 40 gives exactly 72.5, scored 72. ADR 0017 does not say how to round .5. Suggest: state it in ADR 0017 or use half-up.

### T1-W6 (owner W2, info): price-rise tolerance
- `recurring.py:45` `PRICE_RISE_MAX = 0.50`: a single step up of up to 50% counts as a price rise. ADR 0016 says "amount within 10%". A +30% step is accepted as a price rise. The module docstring states the rule. Suggest an ADR note.

### T1-W7 (owner W2, info): emergency fund confidence depends on the seed
- `next_move.py:811`: confidence is a random draw. overspender-bristol: 0.22 at seed 0, 0.28 at seed 7. The API passes a stable per-user seed (`subject_seed`), so one user always sees the same value.
