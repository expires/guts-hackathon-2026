## Failures

### F1. `sample_size` is missing on deterministic moves. Owner: W2 (engine)

- Evidence: on every sample, `GET /v1/next-move` returns `weekly_budget` with `confidence: 1.0`, `confidence_level: "high"`, `confidence_label: "Simple arithmetic on your own numbers, not a forecast"`, and no `sample_size`. The minimal manual profile (no costs, no income) gets the fallback `build_emergency_fund` ("Keep your safety net topped up") with no `sample_size`.
- Cause: `lifeline_engine/next_move.py:348` defaults `sample_size` to `None`. `_weekly_budget` (`:1231`) and `_fallback` (`:1395`) do not pass a value. `level_of(..., deterministic=True)` gives "high" without a sample.
- Conflict: ADR 0053 bug 4 says "high" only with a sufficient, relevant sample, and the app shows `sample_size`. The weekly pace uses 4 weeks of spending (`weeks_seen=4`).
- Fix options (W2 to decide, BOSS to record in an ADR):
  - Set `sample_size = weeks_seen` on `weekly_budget` and `0` on the fallback, or
  - record in an ADR that arithmetic moves have no sample, and make the app show the label instead of a sample size.

### F2. The first sample import after a cold start takes 16 to 26 s. Owner: W4 (api), with W1 (data)

- Evidence: live mode, first import after container start: 16.62 s (scholarship, first probe run) and 26.27 s (commuter, after `docker restart`). Next imports: 0.03 to 0.07 s.
- Cause: `/v1/health` reports ok before `_warm_up` (`lifeline_api/main.py:93`) finishes the MacroTable build. The import calls `compute_next_move` (`lifeline_api/routes/account.py:128`), which waits for the same build. The log shows `live fetch failed for fred/IRLTLT01GBM156N: fred: timeout` during the build.
- Risk: after a Railway deploy or restart, the first judge import can take more than 10 s (ADR 0055 target).
- Fix options: make health or readiness wait for warm-up; do not wait for the MacroTable during import (compute the next move after the response); shorten the FRED timeout (W1).
- Workaround for the demo: after a deploy, run one sample import before the judges start.

### F3. `make test-stack` fails on `test_actions_and_rewind`. Owner: BOSS (contract tests)

- Evidence: `tests/contract/test_contract.py:261`: the second rewind returns 200, the test expects 402. In-process (new DB) the test passes.
- Cause: the stack Postgres still has a `rewind_pass` entitlement on device `contract-test-device` from an earlier run. `lifeline_api/routes/lives.py:239` applies it. The guard at `test_contract.py:324` stops new leaks. It does not remove the old entitlement.
- Fix: use a new device id for each run in `test_actions_and_rewind` (for example, a uuid suffix), or clear entitlements in that test. This failure is not a V2 regression.

## Earlier failures, re-checked

- **F1 (`sample_size` on deterministic moves): closed by decision.** The ADR 0053 amendment (V1, V2 reviews) says:
  - plain-arithmetic moves (cancel_subscription, lifetime_isa) may be "high" with no sample;
  - `weekly_budget` is "medium";
  - the fallback move has no confidence level;
  - "high" needs at least 8 cases.
- The stack follows these rules:
  - `weekly_budget`: `medium`, no `sample_size`.
  - `lifetime_isa` (commuter): `high`, no `sample_size`, label "Based on your own numbers".
  - Fallback "Keep your safety net topped up": no `confidence_level`, no `sample_size`.
  - Every other move has `sample_size` and a level that matches its sample (for example, rich `cut_budget` low with sample 2, working `pay_off_debt` medium with sample 5).
  - My probe's "every move has `sample_size`" assertion predates the amendment, so I did not count its 10 hits.
